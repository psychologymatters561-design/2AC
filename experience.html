Four ways an AC
dies before its time

01 / SKIPPED STEP

No vacuum evacuation

Air and moisture left inside the refrigerant circuit form acids that corrode the compressor from within. Invisible on day one. Fatal by year three. It is the most commonly skipped step in a cheap installation, because no customer can see whether it was done.

Costs you the compressor
02 / SKIPPED STEP

Gas topped up, leak ignored

Refrigerant sits in a sealed loop. It is not consumed, so if it is low, it leaked. Recharging without finding the leak means paying for the same gas next summer, while the system runs starved and the compressor takes the punishment.

Costs you the same bill, twice
03 / SKIPPED STEP

Drainage without fall

A drain line run flat holds water instead of shedding it. It reads fine at handover and reappears a year later as a stain spreading across your ceiling. Nearly every "AC leaking water" call we attend is drainage that was never set correctly.

Costs you the ceiling
04 / SKIPPED STEP

Sized on floor area

A chart says square feet. A west-facing top floor with full glazing says otherwise. Size on the chart and the system runs flat out all afternoon and still loses, or it is oversized, short-cycles, and leaves the room cold and clammy.

Costs you every summer
The standard

What we do instead

None of this is exotic. It is the ordinary correct procedure, done every time, on every job, including the ones nobody will ever inspect. That is the entire difference.

Evacuated, then held

We pull a deep vacuum and hold it, to prove the circuit is tight before a gram of refrigerant goes in. The step that takes the extra hour is the step that buys the extra decade.

Charged by weight

To the manufacturer's stated figure, adjusted for the actual pipe run. Not until the gauge looks about right. Over and under both cost you capacity and compressor life.

Sized on heat load

Orientation, floor level, glazing, ceiling height, occupancy, equipment. We measure the room you actually have, then specify. The survey is free and it is the part that matters most.

Documented, then warranted

Commissioning readings recorded at handover, and a written report after every visit. It is what makes a warranty claim straightforward and an audit uneventful.
